# 07 · Phased implementation plan

Each phase has an exit gate. A phase does not start until the previous gate is passed, or until its failure is documented and accepted.

| Phase | Scope | Exit gate | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 Research & feasibility** | Problem statement, RQs, evaluation criteria, architecture, assumptions register, risks, baseline method, literature matrix | Criteria fixed before results; every simulator number has provenance | **Done** (this package) |
| **1 Working simulation** | Workload, power, thermal, plant models; 5 policies; logging; benchmark; validation tests | Tests pass; energy balance closes; baseline reproducible; ≥2 strategies compared with CIs | **Done**, with a known gap: joint-controller results vary ~0.5% with decision path (E004) |
| **2 Interactive visualisation** | FastAPI service over the store; experiment manager; Three.js lab (cooling, facility, chip, photonic concept, energy flow) bound to registry parameters and stored runs | Every 3-D quantity traceable to a stored run or registry value and labelled conceptual / simulated / measured | **Done.** Plain JavaScript modules with vendored Three.js instead of React/TypeScript (no build step, works offline on Windows); see `03` |
| **3 Joint optimisation, extended** | Dynamic loop model; move suppression; self-correcting controller; *still open:* horizon MPC, routing integrated with clock and cooling, power cap, multi-node placement | Joint vs independent re-run under the dynamic plant; mismatch shows graceful degradation | **Partly done** (E006 v2, E007, E008, E011–E014). Lag-aware planning removed slow-loop overheating in simulation (E011); the ablation E011B separated it from a better overload fallback; A fixed margin did not fix the steady-state controller (E013). The lag-aware controller's safety came from never crediting cooling that had not arrived: it never moved the coolant on slow loops, so E014's loop-model test had no power. Still open: routing with clock and cooling, power cap, multi-node placement |
| **4 Real-hardware validation** | NVML telemetry logger (read-only); calibration sweeps and fits; ingestion as measurements; calibrated experiment versions | Predicted vs measured within stated error on held-out runs; claims limited to the measured boundary | **In progress.** M001/M002 (RTX 5070): clock sensitivity measured and used in E001C; power-vs-clock shape compared with the simulator (error up to 0.09 of top-clock values); voltage–frequency and leakage coefficient rejected; leakage temperature slope measured at two points, about a third of the registry's. E009: with that slope, joint control's energy advantage falls under 0.6% everywhere. E010: its thermal-safety benefit holds with an instant plant but fails under a slow loop in the hottest fouled conditions. Voltage-floor V–f form fitted to M001 (shape error 0.016 vs 0.049). E012: with RTX 5070-derived parameters, a 40 °C supply floor for heat reuse costs 2.7–4.5% energy per task, about a third of the placeholder estimate; warm water is still not the energy optimum. M003 (fixed-fan leakage sweep) tooling ready (`09` §8, `10`, `RESULTS`) |
| **5 Hardware feasibility** | Only if Phase 4 identifies a bottleneck that software and existing parts cannot fix | See gates in `08_hardware_feasibility.md` | Not justified by current evidence |
| **6 Publication** | Paper-format package separating new contributions from prior work | Prior-art review complete for every novelty claim | Not started |

## Immediate next steps (ordered)

1. **M003, leakage at fixed fan speeds (user-run, about 55 minutes).** The leakage slope sets the size of every remaining real-silicon effect: joint control's energy advantage (E009) and the chip-side cost of heat reuse (E012). `python -m ueic calib plan-leak --clock 2347 --fans 100,80,60,45,30` prints the procedure.
2. **Approach-dependent dry-cooler model**, then re-ask E012's question. The current fan model (power proportional to heat rejected) made E012's warm-water hypotheses impossible to pass.
3. **A controller that credits cooling when it arrives.** A multi-step MPC with the loop model, keeping "safety at the warmer of measured supply and setpoint" as a constraint. Pre-register it where the setpoint must move (heavier load, rising ambient, cold start). In E011–E014 the lag-aware controller never moved the coolant, so its loop model was never exercised. Then re-run E001C, E009 and E010 with the best controller, the throughput-first fallback and the voltage-floor V–f form.
4. **Loop-model structure error** (stratification, transport delay) **and unnoticed degradation** (E005/E008 with the new rule).
5. **Routing in the joint optimiser,** plus a facility power-cap scenario and synchronised training load steps (L46).
6. **Operation-level energy model** (FLOPs and bytes per phase) for gate 1 of `08`.
